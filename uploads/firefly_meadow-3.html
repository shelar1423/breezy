Firefly Meadow

Blow out slowly and the firefly floats up. Stop, and it drifts down while you breathe in. Every time you do it, a flower grows.

For grown-ups: this is paced pursed-lip breathing — four seconds out, three seconds in. Everything else in here is optional.

Notes for grown-ups

The breathing is the point. The flute is a bonus. Every mode below is a complete breathing exercise on its own. The flute layer only changes which colour the flowers come out — a child who cannot manage the fingering still finishes the session and still grows a full meadow.

The exercise is pursed-lip breathing: out slowly through pursed lips for about four seconds, in through the nose for about three. Roughly eight breaths a minute. One session is eight breaths, about a minute.

Your recorder is German fingering, which means going down the scale is simply "cover one more finger" each time — B, then A, then G. That is why the on-screen flute fills from the top down, and why a five-year-old can follow it without reading a chart.

Where the note comes from. The laptop microphone listens to the flute and works out which note is sounding. That means no copper tape is needed — the fingering panel works with nothing but a recorder and a laptop. If the copper pads are fitted, they act as a backup for when the room is noisy or the child is not making a sound yet.

There is no losing, no timer pressure and no score that goes down. If a breath is short, the firefly simply doesn't rise as high that time.
